Jailbreaking Prompt Attack: A Controllable Adversarial Attack against Diffusion Models

Abstract

Text-to-image (T2I) models can be maliciously used to generate harmful content such as sexually explicit, unfaithful, and misleading or Not-Safe-for-Work (NSFW) images. Previous attacks largely depend on the availability of the diffusion model or involve a lengthy optimization process. In this work, we investigate a more practical and universal attack that does not require the presence of a target model and demonstrate that the high-dimensional text embedding space inherently contains NSFW concepts that can be exploited to generate harmful images. We present the Jailbreaking Prompt Attack (JPA). JPA first searches for the target malicious concepts in the text embedding space using a group of antonyms generated by ChatGPT. Subsequently, a prefix prompt is optimized in the discrete vocabulary space to align malicious concepts semantically in the text embedding space. We further introduce a soft assignment with gradient masking technique that allows us to perform gradient ascent in the discrete vocabulary space. We perform extensive experiments with open-sourced T2I models and closed-sourced online services with black-box safety checkers. Results show that (1) JPA bypasses both text and image safety checkers (2) while preserving high semantic alignment with the target prompt. (3) JPA demonstrates a much faster speed than previous methods and can be executed in a fully automated manner. These merits render it a valuable tool for robustness evaluation in future text-to-image generation research.

JPA: Jailbreaking Prompt Attack

JPA: Jailbreaking Prompt Attack

In this section, we introduce our Jailbreaking Prompt Attack (JPA), as shown in Figure 4(a). Given a sensitive prompt \( p_t \in \mathcal{P}_\text{sensitive} \), JPA aims to search for an adversarial prompt \( p_a \in \mathcal{P}_\text{insensitive} \).

Denote a target prompt of length \( n \) as \( p_t = [p_1, p_2, \cdots, p_n] \). JPA starts by adding \( k \) learnable tokens at the beginning as the initial prompt: \( p_a = [v_1,...,v_i,...,v_k, p_1, p_2, \cdots, p_n] \). Each learnable token \( v_i \) is first randomly selected from the vocabulary \( V \), such as the CLIP token vocabulary.

We enable the gradient of all \( v_i \) and perform backpropagation to calculate the gradient for optimizing the attack. For each learnable token position \( i \), any token from a vocabulary \( V \) is considered a potential substitute.

Figure 4: (a) Overview of the Jailbreaking Prompt Attack (JPA)... (b) Safety checkers either suppress sensitive prompts or sanitize them. Our insight is to find adversarial prompts that evade detection while preserving NSFW semantics.

We optimize \( p_a \) to align with targeted NSFW concepts. Given a specific concept like "nudity", we generate \( N \) antonym pairs (e.g., "nude" vs "clothed") using ChatGPT-4 and compute:

\[ r = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^n \mathcal{T}(r_i^+) - \mathcal{T}(r_i^-) \]

We then construct the modified prompt embedding: \[ \mathcal{T}(p_r) = \mathcal{T}(p_t) + \lambda \cdot r \] which contains both the semantic fidelity of the original prompt and the NSFW direction.

To retrieve a discrete prompt \( p_a \) from the continuous embedding space, we optimize cosine similarity: \[ \max_{p_a}\frac{\mathcal{T}(p_a)\cdot \mathcal{T}(p_r)}{| \mathcal{T}(p_a)|\cdot| \mathcal{T}(p_r)|} \]

We use PGD (Projected Gradient Descent) for the search, with softmax relaxation over vocabulary: \[ \text{embed}[i] = \sum_{k=1}^L \frac{e^{v_{ik}}}{\sum_{h=1}^L e^{v_{ih}}} E_k \] where \( L \) is vocabulary size, \( E \) is the embedding matrix, and \( v_{ik} \) is the soft assignment.

After optimization, the final token at each position is selected as: \[ v_i = \arg \max_k v_{ik} \]

Since this optimization often yields highly sensitive tokens, we apply gradient masking: for sensitive words, we assign large negative gradients (e.g., 1e9) to avoid their selection. This ensures adversarial prompts retain NSFW semantics without triggering safety mechanisms.

Controllable NSFW Concept Rendering

We discover that by adjusting the magnitude of the NSFW concept embeddings, JPA can precisely control the extent to which NSFW concepts emerge in the generated images. As shown in Figure 2(b), increasing λ leads to more NSFW content being rendered in the image.
